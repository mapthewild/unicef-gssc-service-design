How four GSSC services run today

How I would run the 12-month project

Service Design Investment Project Manager, UNICEF Global Shared Services Centre (GSSC)

The job

Over 12 months, from January 2027 to January 2028, the project has to improve four GSSC services: recruitment, consultant contracts, travel and offboarding. The terms of reference ask for each one to be stabilised, standardised and optimised.

I would coordinate six people. Each of the four services has a project manager, who reports to that service's business owner. A service design advisor compares the GSSC with other shared services centres, and a consultant from Business Innovation and Support turns business requirements into system designs.

When things happen

January and February 2027

Agree with Business Performance and Modernization, the GSSC section that steers the project, what the project has to change and how we will measure it. Ask each service what data it keeps, so we know where each one starts. Set up the project plan and the meetings.

The inception report, which sets out the method and the schedule, is due 15 February. The full project plan is due 26 February.

March to August 2027

Study the four services one after another. For each one, interview the people who run it and the people who use it, then bring a short list of options to a two-day workshop in Budapest, where the team decides what to change.

Small fixes go in as soon as a team agrees on them. The service's project manager is responsible for each fix, and I report on them every month.

The four assessments are due on 15 April, 28 May, 5 July and 20 August.

September 2027

Combine the four assessments into one summary of the changes planned for all four services, and present it to leadership and the Change Review Board, the GSSC board that reviews the project's main reports.

Due 15 September.

September to December 2027

Write the business requirements for any system changes, and review the designs with the Business Innovation and Support consultant. Help each service's project manager write a project plan, and agree who keeps each improvement running after the project ends.

Due 6 December.

January 2028

Hand over the final improvement plan and its benefits case, which sets out what each change is expected to deliver. UNICEF reports efficiency as cost avoidance, time savings and quality improvements, so the benefits case would use those measures.

Due 15 January.

What could get in the way

  • The third assessment needs 30 days of work and has 26 weekdays between 28 May, when the second is due, and 5 July. The September summary needs 20 days and has 18. I would start interviews for the third service before the second workshop, and start the summary during the fourth assessment.
  • A change management contract runs from November 2026 to October 2027 and plans "engagement with approximately 60 stakeholders", and a study of the benefits that GSSC services deliver was advertised in March 2026. If they involve the same people, I would agree with both teams who interviews whom, so nobody answers the same questions twice.
  • For the whole GSSC, UNICEF publishes 96.5% average client satisfaction and 92.5% service level compliance in 2025. I found no figures for each service. Each service's starting figures would come from its data in the first month.
  • The Board of Auditors recommended that the Digital Impact Division automate eligibility checks for consultants. Fixes like that need another division's time, so I would ask for their dates early.

Questions for the first week

  1. By January 2028, do you expect the changes to be live, or is an approved, costed plan enough?
  2. Who signs off each deliverable, and when does the Change Review Board meet?
  3. Did the March 2026 benefits study produce a method this project can use?
  4. How do we split interviews with the change management team?
  5. What data does each service keep today?
  6. Which service goes first, and in what order do the others follow?
