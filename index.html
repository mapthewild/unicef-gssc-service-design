How four GSSC services run today

These maps show how UNICEF's Global Shared Services Centre (GSSC) in Budapest runs four of its services: recruitment, consultant contracts, travel and offboarding.

  • Each map is a service blueprint: what the user does in the top row, what GSSC staff do below it, and the systems underneath.
  • I built them from public documents: job postings, audit reports, UN rules and the GSSC's annual report. I have not spoken to anyone at UNICEF, so every step needs checking with the team that runs it once the project starts.
  • "To confirm" marks a step the documents don't describe. System names are as each document gives them, so two names may mean the same system.

I made this as an applicant for the Service Design Investment Project Manager consultancy. UNICEF did not ask for it and has not endorsed it.

The GSSC's recruitment team is called Recruitment Administrative Services. Its users are hiring offices and their managers, interview panels, People and Culture business partners, and candidates. The team follows UNICEF's 2024 staff selection procedure, and is growing from a pilot to cover offices worldwide.

The GSSC's consultant contracts team is called Individual Consultant Services. Its users are contracting managers in country offices, supervisors and consultants. The team follows the Division of People and Culture's procedures for individual consultants.

1. Advertise and select
2. Eligibility
3. Review committee
4. Offer and contract
5. Deliver and invoice
6. Payment
7. Changes and closure
UserWhat the user does
  • The office advertises the consultancy, and consultants quote a fee for each deliverable
  • Consultants are paid per deliverable and work remotely by default
  • The consultant fills in a self-declaration form
  • To confirm
  • The consultant gets an offer and a contract
  • The consultant shares deliverables with the supervisor, then submits an invoice through the Consultant Portal
  • Invoices sent before the work is accepted are rejected
  • The GSSC tells consultants to expect approval within five working days, then payment in the office's next payment run. Offices run payments two to five times a week
  • To update a vendor record, or for anything the portal can't do, the consultant asks the focal point at their UNICEF office
Line of interaction
FrontstageWhat GSSC does that the user sees
  • The team reviews terms of reference and supports sourcing and selection
  • To confirm
  • To confirm
  • The team prepares offers and issues contracts
  • The GSSC's consultant page gives guidance and invoice templates
  • The Consultant Portal shows the progress of each invoice
  • The team processes requests to change a contract
Line of visibility
BackstageWhat GSSC does out of sight
  • The team has service level targets and performance indicators, with shifts from Sunday to Friday
  • To confirm
  • Before this team existed, eligibility was checked by hand and through the self-declaration form
  • Contracts and amendments above $100,000 (country and regional offices) or $150,000 (headquarters) go to the Contracts Review Committee
  • The contract is set up as a purchase order, with a vendor record for the consultant
  • The supervisor reviews the invoice after a first check
  • Payment goes out in the office's regular payment run
  • The team follows up expired contracts and closes them
